# Nationwide Permit Source Coverage

## Operating Model

Permit data is published by cities and counties, not uniformly by states. A
state is considered covered only when at least one high-value jurisdiction has
an official, current, bulk source with stable identity, actionable project
context, and affirmative commercial reuse terms. Issued-only feeds are useful
confirmation layers but do not satisfy early-warning coverage by themselves.

Status meanings:

- `production`: checked-in source with pre-approval and approved records.
- `confirmation`: checked-in issued/approved source without broad early stages.
- `audit`: live source verification in progress.
- `legal hold`: technically suitable, but commercial reuse is not affirmatively
  licensed.
- `technical hold`: useful public records exist, but no stable bulk interface,
  durable identity, or safe reconciliation contract is available.
- `freshness hold`: a once-useful source is too delayed or stale for early
  alerts.
- `lifecycle hold`: official data exists but does not expose useful pre-approval
  records.
- `queued`: target jurisdictions selected for the next audit loop.

## Expansion Order

1. Every state now has an explicit production or candidate-catalog decision;
   40 states have production sources and 10 are candidate-only. This pass added
   official-source holds for the 10 states that previously had no catalog
   representation; Georgia has now moved from candidate-only to production.
   Re-audit those holds
   when publishers add licensed bulk feeds or materially change platforms.
2. Run parcel-source admission loops in production permit markets, starting
   with Austin/Travis County, Seattle/King County, and New York City tax lots.
3. Continue in three-state loops, onboarding only sources that pass technical,
   lifecycle, freshness, identity, and licensing gates.
4. Pursue written permissions in parallel for Orlando, Raleigh, Phoenix, and
   Atlanta while monitoring Savannah's weekly production reconciliation; a
   legal hold should not consume connector engineering time.
5. Add secondary jurisdictions after the first production-grade source in a
   state, prioritizing population growth, retail expansion, and data quality.

## First Three Rollout Clusters

The first pass through the expansion loop should work in state clusters, not
individual one-off sources. That keeps the team focused on repeatable source
patterns, cleaner legal review, and faster graph payoff.

| Cluster | States / markets | Why this comes next | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Texas, Washington, New York | These markets already have the deepest production footholds, the strongest early-warning mix, and the clearest path from permit to graph to opportunity. They are the fastest way to deepen the live coverage spine and keep the landing page fed with high-signal activity. | At least one additional production-grade source or certified retry per state, plus a verified parcel spine and graph linkage. |
| 2 | California, North Carolina, Florida | These states combine retail velocity, multiple known source families, and a high chance of surfacing chain motion before formal approval. They are the best follow-on batch for broadening national chain detection without giving up signal quality. | At least one live pre-approval source and one parcel-context source in each state, with approved-only confirmation kept in reserve. |
| 3 | Colorado, Massachusetts, Maryland | These markets are structurally strong, have clean lifecycle data, and can usually be expanded with low connector friction. They are the right next step for turning a few dense markets into a repeatable template before deeper hold-state work. | One new production-grade source or retry-activated candidate per state, with freshness and identity checks stable enough to promote the state into the active rotation. |

Each cluster should be executed as a loop:

1. Confirm rights and admissibility.
2. Implement or update the connector.
3. Run a canary or backfill validation.
4. Verify canonical mapping, entity resolution, and evidence retention.
5. Promote the source into the live coverage map and the graph.

Coverage breadth is not a reason to weaken provenance. Every onboarded record
must continue to retain the official source, raw evidence, exact lifecycle
status, approval stage, observed timestamp, and verification health.

The dashboard and Source Health page now surface a 50-state coverage gap view.
That view counts states with at least one live or candidate source and lists the
remaining states that still need a verified source. It is an operating aid, not
a claim that every jurisdiction in a state is fully covered.

The same surfaces also show the strongest covered states by live and candidate
source count, which helps the expansion loop pick the next cluster of adjoining
jurisdictions rather than treating the map as a flat queue.

The coverage response also includes a 50-state rollout queue. It preserves the
existing jurisdiction footprint, assigns the first nine states to the three
named rollout clusters, and places every remaining state in the nationwide
expansion queue. Each state receives one current action: run a candidate canary,
resolve a candidate blocker, add pre-approval coverage, add retailer-opening
coverage, add a secondary jurisdiction, or discover its first official source.

States that already have candidate coverage but no live source are called out
separately as the next activation queue. That makes it easier to move a source
from research into production without losing the broader nationwide picture.

Those state chips now link into the Source Health page with a state filter
applied, so operators can jump from the coverage map straight into the relevant
source rows and candidate worklist.
